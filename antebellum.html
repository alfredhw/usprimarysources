AmeriDocs

Online Sources for United States History
Home › Antebellum America, 1815–1861

Sources from the market revolution through the eve of the Civil War: expansion, reform, and the deepening sectional crisis. See also Slavery & Abolition, Westward Expansion, and Women’s History.

The Market Revolution & Transportation

  • Library of Congress — Railroad Maps, 1828–1900 700+ maps documenting the rise of the American railroad network: construction proposals, land-grant maps, and route surveys from the earliest lines forward.
  • New York Heritage — Erie Canal Collection Digitized primary sources (1792–early 1900s) on Erie Canal construction and operation: reports, financial records, cargo inventories, maps, and travelers’ accounts from the Erie Canal Museum. (See also Mid-Atlantic.)
  • DPLA — The Steam Engine and Transportation Curated images, patents, and documents illustrating steamboats, railroads, and the transportation revolution’s effect on commerce and westward expansion.

Jacksonian Democracy & Politics

  • DPLA — Jacksonian Democracy Documents, cartoons, and images spanning Jackson’s presidency (1829–1837): the spoils system, the Bank War, nullification, and the expansion of white male suffrage.
  • Library of Congress — Martin Van Buren Papers 6,000+ digitized items (bulk 1820s–1840s) documenting party organization and Jacksonian-era politics from Van Buren’s years as secretary of state, vice president, and president.

Indian Removal & the Trail of Tears

  • Library of Congress — Indian Removal Act Digital Collections Government documents, manuscripts, congressional debates, maps, and printed ephemera related to the 1830 Indian Removal Act and its aftermath. (See also Native Nations.)
  • DPLA — Cherokee Removal and the Trail of Tears Curated primary sources (1803–1897): treaty excerpts, John Ross correspondence, Cherokee Phoenix pages, and Jackson’s 1835 letter to the Cherokee Nation, with teaching guide.
  • Digital Library of Georgia — Cherokee Phoenix Digitized runs of the Cherokee Phoenix (1828–1834), the first Native American newspaper, printed in both Cherokee syllabary and English, with related removal-era records.

Abolitionism & the Slavery Debate

  • Documenting the American South — North American Slave Narratives 344 digitized and transcribed autobiographical slave narratives and biographies published through 1920—the largest such collection in the world. (See also Slavery & Abolition.)
  • Cornell — Samuel J. May Anti-Slavery Pamphlet Collection 10,000+ digitized pamphlets, sermons, speeches, and Anti-Slavery Society newsletters (1820s–1860s), plus manuscripts from Garrison, Lydia Maria Child, and Wendell Phillips.
  • National Archives — The Amistad Case Digitized federal court records from the 1839–1841 Amistad mutiny case, including the habeas corpus petition and Supreme Court documents.

Reform Movements

  • DPLA — Women of the Antebellum Reform Movement Documents and images (1820s–1850s) on Dorothea Dix’s asylum campaigns, temperance activism, and education reform: letters, legislative testimony, and period journalism.
  • DPLA — Nineteenth-Century Schools for the Deaf and Blind Primary sources documenting antebellum institutional education reform and pedagogy for disabled students in the Horace Mann era.

Seneca Falls & Early Women’s Rights

  • Library of Congress — Seneca Falls Convention Proceedings (1848) Digitized original printed proceedings of the July 1848 convention containing the full Declaration of Sentiments, preserved in Elizabeth Cady Stanton’s scrapbooks. (See also Women’s History.)
  • Library of Congress — Stanton & Anthony Papers Correspondence, diaries, speeches, and organizational records documenting the founding of the women’s rights movement from 1848 through Reconstruction.

The Mexican-American War

  • UT Arlington — A Continent Divided: The US–Mexico War Digitized primary sources from the 10,000+ item Jenkins Garrett Collection: proclamations, soldiers’ letters, diaries, broadsides, maps, sheet music, and newspapers documenting the 1846–1848 conflict. (See also US & Latin America.)
  • National Archives — Mexican War Military Records Muster rolls, pension files, and campaign correspondence relating to US Army operations in the Mexican-American War (1846–1848).

Bleeding Kansas & the Sectional Crisis

  • Kansas Historical Society — Kansas Memory 764,000+ digitized items including the Bleeding Kansas (1854–1861) collection: territorial government records, newspapers, manuscripts, and photographs documenting the Kansas-Nebraska Act’s violent aftermath. (See also Great Plains.)
  • University of Kansas — Kansas Collection Digitized manuscripts and government records covering the territorial period (1854–1861), continuing the cooperative Territorial Kansas Online project.

Westward Migration

  • BYU — Trails of Hope: Overland Diaries and Letters, 1846–1869 49 diarists’ original writings from the Mormon, California, Oregon, and Montana trails, with 82 photographs and sketches, 43 maps, and seven trail guides. (See also Westward Expansion.)
  • Oregon-California Trails Association — Overland Journals Digital copies of thousands of original overland trail diaries and letters, plus the Paper Trail database of 3,500+ trail documents and 74,000 names.

Religious Ferment & Utopian Communities

  • The Joseph Smith Papers Comprehensive free digital publication of Joseph Smith’s writings, revelations, journals, letters, and legal records (1820s–1844), with complete transcriptions and scholarly annotation. (See also Religious History.)
  • Hamilton College — Communal Societies Collection The world’s largest collection on American communal societies: primary materials on the Shakers, Oneida, Amana, and other antebellum utopian and religious communities, with unrestricted access.
