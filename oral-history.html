Online Sources for United States History
Home › Oral History

Recorded testimony as primary source: the voices of Americans describing their own lives, from the last generation born in slavery to yesterday’s veterans.

National Collections

  • Library of Congress — Veterans History Project 121,000+ firsthand accounts from American war veterans and civilian war workers (WWI to present): audio and video interviews, letters, diaries, and photographs collected by the American Folklife Center. (See also Military & Wartime.)
  • StoryCorps Archive 425,000+ recorded conversations between everyday Americans since 2003, permanently preserved at the Library of Congress—the largest single collection of human voices ever gathered.
  • Library of Congress — Civil Rights History Project 100+ video oral histories with veterans of the civil rights movement, recorded 2010–2011 with the Smithsonian NMAAHC: SNCC, SCLC, and grassroots organizers. (See also African American Experience.)
  • Smithsonian NMAAHC — Oral History Initiative Ongoing interviews with elders and cultural figures of African American history, searchable through the museum’s online collection catalog.

Voices of Slavery & the WPA Recordings

  • Library of Congress — Voices Remembering Slavery 23 audio interviews (recorded 1932–1975) with formerly enslaved people, made by folklorists including Zora Neale Hurston and the Lomaxes—the only firsthand audio testimony of American slavery.
  • Library of Congress — American Life Histories (WPA) Approximately 2,900 life-history narratives (1936–1940) transcribed by Depression-era Federal Writers’ Project interviewers—among them Ralph Ellison—documenting ordinary lives, immigration, and labor. (See also World Wars & Depression.)
  • American Folklife Center — Occupational Folklife Project 1,800+ interviews (2010–present) with contemporary American workers across dozens of trades—crop dusters, circus workers, home health aides—continuing the WPA tradition. (See also Labor & Industry.)

Community Memory Projects

  • Densho — Oral History Collection Filmed interviews with survivors of WWII Japanese American incarceration, redress activists, and descendants—nearly 2,000 hours, fully indexed and transcribed. (See also Asian American Experience.)
  • USC Shoah Foundation — Visual History Archive 60,000+ video testimonies from survivors and witnesses of the Holocaust and other genocides, with free-access portions and educational excerpts via IWitness.
  • Bracero History Archive Oral histories and artifacts of the Bracero guest worker program (1942–1964), compiled by George Mason, the Smithsonian, Brown, and UTEP’s Institute of Oral History. (See also Hispanic & Latino Experience.)
  • September 11 Digital Archive 150,000+ digital items including 40,000+ first-hand stories and emails documenting September 11 and its aftermath. (See also Recent America.)

University Programs

  • UNC Chapel Hill — Southern Oral History Program 6,000+ interviews since 1973 documenting the American South: activists, millworkers, educators, and everyday people, freely searchable through the Southern Historical Collection. (See also Southeast.)
  • Columbia University — Center for Oral History Research The nation’s oldest oral history program (founded 1948): thousands of interviews on twentieth-century American political, cultural, and social history.
  • UCLA — Center for Oral History Research 2,000+ in-depth interviews on Southern California history, social movements, communities of color, and the arts, with many transcripts and recordings online. (See also California.)
  • University of Kentucky — Louie B. Nunn Center for Oral History 17,000+ interviews on Kentucky and Appalachian history, civil rights, agriculture, and industry, searchable with OHMS-synchronized transcripts and audio. (See also Kentucky.)
  • Baylor University — Institute for Oral History Thousands of interviews since 1970 on Texas history, religion, and community life, with streaming audio and transcripts.

State & Regional Collections

  • Minnesota Historical Society — Oral History Collection 1,600+ interviews since 1948 on Minnesota politics, labor, agriculture, and immigrant communities, fully digitized with streamable audio and transcripts. (See also Minnesota.)
  • Wisconsin Veterans Museum — Oral History Collection 2,800+ interviews with Wisconsin-connected veterans from the Spanish-American War through present conflicts, with audio, video, and transcripts online. (See also Wisconsin.)
  • University of Alaska Fairbanks — Project Jukebox Place-based oral history “jukeboxes” pairing streamable interviews with maps, photos, and documents on Alaska Native communities, mining, fishing, and rural life. (See also Alaska.)

Practice & Method

  • Oral History Association The principal US professional organization for oral historians: best-practice guidelines, ethics statements, and a directory linking to dozens of university and museum programs nationwide.
