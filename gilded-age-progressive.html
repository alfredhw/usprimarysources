Home › Gilded Age & Progressive Era, 1877–1917

Sources for the age of industrialization, immigration, and reform. See also Labor & Industry, Immigration, and Economic & Commercial History.

Immigration in the Ellis Island Era

  • Statue of Liberty–Ellis Island Foundation — Arrival Records Free searchable database of passenger arrival records for Ellis Island and other US ports: ship manifests spanning the eighteenth to twentieth centuries, with an accompanying oral history project. (See also Immigration.)
  • Harvard — Immigration to the United States, 1789–1930 400,000+ pages from 2,200+ books and pamphlets plus manuscripts documenting voluntary immigration: diaries, legislative acts, and union records with strong Gilded Age coverage.

Urbanization & the Tenement City

  • Library of Congress — Jacob Riis: Revealing “How the Other Half Lives” Digitized text and photographs from Riis’s landmark 1890 tenement exposé, plus related lantern slides and manuscripts documenting Lower East Side slum conditions.
  • Museum of the City of New York — Jacob A. Riis Collection The world’s largest Riis photographic archive (1,000+ images), sole repository of his original negatives documenting New York tenement life, sweatshops, and street children in the 1880s–90s.
  • Library of Congress — National Child Labor Committee Collection Approximately 5,100 Lewis Hine photographs (1908–1924) documenting child laborers in mines, mills, and factories nationwide, taken to support Progressive labor reform.

Labor Conflict

  • Chicago History Museum — Haymarket Affair Digital Collection 3,800+ digitized manuscripts, broadsides, photographs, and artifacts on the 1886 Haymarket bombing, trial, executions, and pardons, including the full searchable conspiracy trial transcript. (See also Labor & Industry.)
  • Cornell — Kheel Center for Labor-Management Documentation 20,000+ linear feet of manuscripts, 400,000 photographs, and oral histories on US labor history: strikes, unions, and management records from the Gilded Age forward.
  • Illinois Labor History Society Archive and resource hub preserving Illinois labor history, with extensive Haymarket primary source guides and regional strike documentation.

Populism & Agrarian Revolt

  • Kansas Memory — Populism Collections Papers of Populist governors, People’s Party platforms, and Populist newspapers central to the 1890s agrarian revolt, among 764,000+ digitized items. (See also Great Plains.)

Jim Crow & Plessy v. Ferguson

  • Civil Rights Digital Library Portal aggregating digitized records including the Plessy v. Ferguson case file, Jim Crow legal documents, and related archival materials with contextual essays. (See also Slavery & Abolition.)
  • National Archives — Judgment in Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) Digitized Supreme Court judgment establishing “separate but equal,” presented with teaching tools via NARA’s DocsTeach platform.
  • Library of Congress — Born in Slavery: FWP Slave Narratives 2,300+ first-person interviews recorded in the 1930s recounting Reconstruction and Jim Crow-era life across seventeen states.

Settlement Houses & Social Reform

  • Jane Addams Digital Edition Scholarly digital edition of Jane Addams’s correspondence and writings (1901–1935)—the core primary-source hub for Hull House and the settlement movement.
  • UIC — Hull-House Museum Digital Collections Digitized manuscripts, photographs, and records from Hull House’s founding era, documenting settlement work, immigrant aid, and reform activism in Chicago.

Conservation & the National Parks

  • Theodore Roosevelt Center Digital Library 64,000+ digitized items—correspondence, diaries, speeches, photographs, and political cartoons—covering TR’s presidency, conservation policy, and national parks advocacy, built with LOC and NPS partnership. (See also Mountain West.)

Suffrage Revival & the Women’s Club Movement

  • Library of Congress — NAWSA Collection Nearly 800 books and pamphlets from Carrie Chapman Catt’s NAWSA library, plus the companion NAWSA Records (26,700+ items, 1839–1961), documenting the suffrage campaign. (See also Women’s History.)

The Spanish-American War & Empire

  • Library of Congress — The World of 1898 Hispanic Division collection with chronologies for Spain, Cuba, Puerto Rico, the Philippines, and the US: newspapers, early films, and manuscript collections (Leonard Wood, Clara Barton) on the war and the empire debate. (See also US & Latin America.)

Newspapers & Muckraking Journalism

  • Library of Congress — Chronicling America 20 million+ searchable newspaper pages covering Gilded Age political, labor, and social life in nearly every state and territory, free with no account required.
  • HathiTrust Digital Library 17 million+ volumes including full-text-searchable Progressive Era books, government reports, and original muckraking periodicals such as McClure’s, most public domain and freely downloadable.
