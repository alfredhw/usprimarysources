AmeriDocs

Online Sources for United States History
Home › World Wars & Depression, 1917–1945

Sources from American entry into the First World War through victory in the Second: mobilization, boom, collapse, and the New Deal. See also Military & Wartime and Economic & Commercial History.

World War I: Mobilization & Propaganda

  • Library of Congress — World War I Posters Approximately 1,900 digitized posters (1914–1920) from the US and allied and enemy nations, including Committee on Public Information mobilization art, freely downloadable.
  • Northwestern University — WWI & WWII Poster Collections Digitized US government propaganda posters from the Government Information Collection, including a 338-item WWII set and WWI holdings.

WWI Soldiers & Veterans

  • Library of Congress — Veterans History Project 121,000+ individual veteran collections spanning WWI through recent conflicts: audio and video interviews, letters, diaries, photographs, and memoirs, all searchable online. (See also Military & Wartime.)

The 1918 Influenza Pandemic

  • University of Michigan — American Influenza Epidemic of 1918 Digital encyclopedia with 50,000+ digitized pages documenting the pandemic across fifty US communities: correspondence, agency reports, newspaper accounts, military records, diaries, and photographs.

The 1920s: Harlem Renaissance, Prohibition & the Scopes Trial

  • NYPL — Digital Schomburg Online exhibitions, manuscripts, and archival media from the Schomburg Center’s Harlem Renaissance holdings, including materials from Arturo Schomburg and Alain Locke. (See also African American Experience.)
  • UMKC — Famous Trials Original trial transcripts, chronologies, and images from fifty-plus landmark US trials including the 1925 Scopes “Monkey” Trial, compiled by law professor Douglas Linder.

The Great Depression & the New Deal

  • FDR Presidential Library — FRANKLIN Digital Repository 800,000+ pages of archival documents and 2,500 photographs from the Roosevelt Library, covering the Depression, New Deal policy, and WWII-era decision-making, in a free virtual research room.
  • Library of Congress — FSA/OWI Photographs Approximately 172,000 black-and-white and 1,600 color photographs (1935–1944) by Lange, Evans, Rothstein, and others, documenting rural poverty and wartime mobilization. (See also Economic History.)
  • Library of Congress — American Life Histories (Federal Writers’ Project) About 2,900 life-history documents compiled by 300+ WPA writers across twenty-four states (1936–1940), capturing first-person accounts of work, folklore, and daily life.
  • Library of Congress — WPA Posters New Deal-era silkscreen and lithograph posters (1936–1943) spanning public health, theater, education, and wartime civil defense.

The Dust Bowl

  • Library of Congress — Voices from the Dust Bowl Ethnographic field recordings, photographs, and documents from FSA migrant labor camps in California (1940–1941), capturing Dust Bowl refugee voices, music, and daily life. (See also Great Plains.)

The WWII Home Front

  • National Archives — Powers of Persuasion Online exhibit analyzing US government WWII propaganda posters on patriotism, rationing, and war production, with contextual essays.
  • National WWII Museum — Digital Collections Thousands of oral histories and photographs covering the home front—rationing, war bonds, women workers—and combat theaters, free to view online.

WWII Military Records

  • National Archives — World War II Records Central gateway to WWII service records, unit histories, enlistment records, draft cards, and after-action reports. (See also Military & Wartime.)

Japanese American Incarceration

  • Densho Digital Repository 100,000+ items including 900+ fully transcribed oral histories, photographs, letters, diaries, and government papers documenting Japanese American incarceration and redress. (See also Asian American Experience.)
  • Calisphere — Japanese American Relocation Digital Archive 20,000+ documents and images aggregated from California institutions: personal diaries, War Relocation Authority camp records, and internee personal histories.

Holocaust Response & Refugees

  • US Holocaust Memorial Museum — Collections Search Searchable database of survivor testimonies, photographs, and documents, with specialized databases on refugee committees and displaced-persons camps documenting US and international response (1938–1950).

The Manhattan Project

  • Voices of the Manhattan Project Approximately 600 audio and video interviews with Manhattan Project scientists, workers, and their families—including Oppenheimer and Groves—plus atomic bomb survivor testimony from Japan. (See also Southwest.)
