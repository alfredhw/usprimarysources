The visual record: photographs and moving images from the daguerreotype era to the newsreel age.

Major Photographic Archives

  • Library of Congress — Prints & Photographs Online Catalog The master catalog: 1 million+ digitized images spanning the 1600s to today—photographs, fine prints, posters, and architectural drawings—the central search hub for nearly every LOC photo collection.
  • National Archives — Still Pictures 14 million+ analog and 3 million digital federal photographs spanning the Civil War through NASA: New Deal, WWII, civil rights, and agency photography, searchable in the National Archives Catalog.
  • Smithsonian Online Virtual Archives Nearly 27,000 archival finding aids with digitized photographs, oral histories, and films across seventeen Smithsonian repositories, including the Anacostia Community Museum and National Anthropological Archives.

Documentary Photography

  • Library of Congress — FSA/OWI Negatives Approximately 175,000 negatives (1935–1944) documenting the Depression and WWII home front: the iconic work of Dorothea Lange, Walker Evans, Gordon Parks, and their colleagues. (See also World Wars & Depression.)
  • National Archives — DOCUMERICA 15,000+ color photographs (1971–1977) commissioned by the EPA to document environmental conditions and everyday American life in the early seventies.
  • Library of Congress — Ansel Adams at Manzanar Approximately 170 photographs (1943–1944) documenting Japanese American incarceration at Manzanar War Relocation Center. (See also Asian American Experience.)

Commercial & News Photography

  • Library of Congress — Detroit Publishing Company Approximately 25,000 photochrom prints and negatives (ca. 1880–1929) of American cities, landscapes, resorts, and industry from the era’s leading commercial view publisher.
  • Library of Congress — Bain News Service Nearly 40,000 glass negatives (bulk 1900s–1920s) from America’s earliest news picture agency: sports, politics, suffrage, disasters, and daily life, with heavy New York coverage.

Early Photography

  • Library of Congress — Civil War Glass Negatives Thousands of wet-plate negatives (1861–1865) including Mathew Brady studio portraits and battlefield scenes—the first systematic photographic documentation of American war. (See also Civil War & Reconstruction.)
  • Library of Congress — Daguerreotypes Hundreds of daguerreotypes (1839–1864), largely from the Brady studio: statesmen, writers, and unidentified sitters from photography’s first American decades.
  • Harvard — Daguerreotypes at Harvard 3,500+ daguerreotypes across Harvard libraries and museums, including the Horblit Collection of Early Photography, with early lunar images and portraiture.
  • NYPL — Robert N. Dennis Stereoscopic Views 72,000+ stereoscopic views (1850s–1910s) of American cities, landscapes, industry, and daily life—the Victorian era’s dominant popular photographic format.
  • Library of Congress — Panoramic Photographs Approximately 4,000 panoramic prints (1851–1991) of cityscapes, expositions, military units, and landscapes shot at extreme horizontal scale.

Regional Photo Archives

  • Cleveland Memory Project Historic photographs including the Cleveland Press photo morgue, oral histories, and yearbooks documenting the Cleveland area from the nineteenth century onward. (See also Ohio.)
  • Los Angeles Public Library — Tessa Digital Collections 116,000+ images anchored by the Security Pacific National Bank Collection and Shades of L.A. family photographs, documenting Los Angeles from the late 1800s. (See also California.)
  • Denver Public Library — Western History Digital Collections A million+ items including historic photographs of Denver and the American West from the library’s renowned Western History and Genealogy Department. (See also Mountain West.)

Moving Images

  • Library of Congress — National Screening Room Free streaming of hundreds of historically significant films (1890s–1980s): early actualities, industrial films, and newsreel footage from the Library’s moving image collections.
  • Internet Archive — Prelinger Archives Thousands of freely streamable ephemeral films—advertising, educational, industrial, and amateur works (1927–1987)—a landmark resource for twentieth-century American visual culture.
  • National Archives — Universal Newsreel Collection Nearly 6,000 edited newsreel reels and 8,500 outtake reels (1929–1967)—the largest newsreel collection at NARA, in the public domain.
  • University of South Carolina — Fox Movietone News Newsreel outtakes and released stories (1919–1944) from one of the earliest sound newsreel producers, digitized by USC’s Moving Image Research Collections.
  • American Archive of Public Broadcasting 40+ years of American public television and radio preserved by the Library of Congress and GBH: news, documentary, and cultural programming freely streamable. (See also Entertainment & Sports.)

Home Movies & Amateur Film

  • Texas Archive of the Moving Image Streaming archive of Texas-related film and video from the early twentieth century onward: home movies, newsreels, advertisements, and educational films. (See also Texas.)
  • Center for Home Movies Nonprofit hub for amateur film preservation, organizer of Home Movie Day, with resources and links to digitized home movie collections documenting everyday twentieth-century life.
  • Travel Film Archive Travelogue and industrial films (1900–1970) from filmmakers including Burton Holmes, browsable by place, subject, and decade.
