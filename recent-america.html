Sources for the post-Cold War decades: the born-digital era, in which the archive itself changed shape. Web archives, digital memory banks, and fully digital presidential libraries.

The Gulf War

  • George H.W. Bush Library — Gulf War Gallery Presidential correspondence, military planning documents, and materials from Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm (1990–1991). (See also Military & Wartime.)
  • National Security Archive — The Iraq Project Approximately 1,900 declassified documents (nearly 10,000 pages) on US-Iraq policy before the Gulf War, plus electronic briefing books of cables, memos, and intelligence reports through 2011.

The Clinton Years

  • Clinton Digital Library Approximately 2 million pages of archival documents, 400+ audio recordings, photographs, and streaming video from the Clinton administration (1993–2001), including declassified NSC files.

The Early Internet

  • Internet Archive — Wayback Machine 150 billion+ archived web pages since the mid-1990s—the foundational born-digital record of the early commercial web, including 1996 presidential campaign sites captured with the Smithsonian.
  • Library of Congress — Web Archives Dozens of curated born-digital collections (elections, legislative branch, public policy) preserved since the late 1990s, free-searchable after a standard embargo.

September 11

  • September 11 Digital Archive 150,000+ born-digital items: 40,000+ emails, 40,000+ first-hand stories, 15,000+ images, and FDNY documents, built by George Mason and CUNY and acquired by the Library of Congress in 2003.
  • Library of Congress — September 11, 2001 Web Archive Government, press, and advocacy websites captured September–December 2001 in 30+ languages, documenting global web reaction to the attacks.
  • National Archives — 9/11 Commission Records Approximately 570 cubic feet of Commission records including searchable Memorandums for the Record from 1,200+ fact-finding interviews.
  • University of North Texas — 9/11 Commission CyberCemetery The Commission’s final report, hearing video and transcripts, and related terrorism reports preserved as permanent public web archives.

Iraq & Afghanistan

  • National Security Archive — Afghanistan-Taliban Project Thousands of FOIA documents on the Afghanistan War (1998–2021), including “Afghanistan 20/20: The 20-Year War in 20 Documents” and materials underlying the “Afghanistan Papers.”

Hurricane Katrina

  • Hurricane Digital Memory Bank The largest free public archive of Katrina and Rita (2005): 25,000+ items including survivor stories, oral histories, photographs, and video, organized by George Mason and the University of New Orleans. (See also Louisiana.)

The Bush & Obama Presidencies

  • George W. Bush Library — Digital Research Room 80 terabytes of electronic records (2001–2009): 200 million emails, approximately 70 million pages of paper records, 30,000 audiovisual recordings, and 3.8 million photographs.
  • Obama Presidential Library — Digital Research Room NARA’s first fully digital presidential library: roughly 95% of records (2009–2017) born-digital, over 250 terabytes including approximately 300 million emails, freely searchable.

The 2008 Financial Crisis

  • Stanford Law — Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission Archive Searchable archive of FCIC materials: interview recordings and transcripts from 700+ witnesses, hearing videos, and staff data projects behind the 2011 final report. (See also Economic History.)
  • National Archives — FCIC Records Finding Aid Finding aid (opened 2016) to Commission records: meeting minutes, interview summaries and transcripts from the 2007–2009 crisis investigation.

Marriage Equality

  • GLBT Historical Society — Marriage and Commitment Curated set of digitized wedding photographs, oral histories, protest signs, and news coverage (1970s–2015), including the 2004 San Francisco City Hall weddings. (See also Gender & Sexuality.)
  • Library of Congress — American Foundation for Equal Rights Web Archive Archived captures of AFER’s site, the organization behind the Proposition 8 federal trial (2009–2013) that helped pave the way to Obergefell, including case timelines and legal filings.

Social Movements in the Digital Age

  • Case Western Reserve — Occupy Archive 1,200+ pages of documentation and 400+ items from Occupy Wall Street (2011–): organizer interviews, protest ephemera, signs, and art.
  • Washington University — Documenting Ferguson Community- and media-generated photographs, video, audio, and stories from the Ferguson protests, launched days after Michael Brown’s killing in August 2014—an early model of movement rapid-response archiving.
  • Harvard Schlesinger Library — #MeToo Digital Media Collection Born-digital collection (2007–2022): 32 million+ tweets, 1,100+ webpages, and news and legal documents documenting #MeToo from origin through legislative aftermath. (See also Women’s History.)

COVID-19

  • A Journal of the Plague Year: An Archive of COVID-19 Crowd-sourced international archive led by Arizona State University historians: photographs, video and audio diaries, memes, and oral histories collected since March 2020.
  • Library of Congress — Coronavirus Web Archive Government, science, economic, and community websites captured March 2020–November 2023 in 20+ languages, including African American, Asian American, Latino, and Native American community content.

Contemporary Oral History

  • Library of Congress — Veterans History Project 121,000+ veteran collections spanning WWI through current conflicts: interviews, letters, diaries, memoirs, and photographs, established by Congress in 2000.
  • StoryCorps Archive Tens of thousands of full-length conversational interviews on everyday American life recorded since 2003, permanently preserved at the Library of Congress.
