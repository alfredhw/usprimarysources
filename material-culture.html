Online Sources for United States History
Home › Objects & Material Culture

Physical objects as primary sources: the things Americans made, used, wore, and carried. Material culture is often the only primary source for people who left few documents—the enslaved, the poor, and communities whose paper trail was written by others.

Smithsonian Collections

  • Smithsonian Open Access 5.1 million+ 2D images and 3D models released CC0—public domain, no permission needed—from all Smithsonian museums and archives: everyday objects, patent models, quilts, portraits, and iconic artifacts from the Wright Flyer to Amelia Earhart’s plane.
  • National Museum of American History — Collections Search 1.7 million+ objects spanning colonial times to today: advertising, medicine, military history, entertainment, the National Quilt Collection, and the National Numismatic Collection, with 3D scans of select items.
  • Smithsonian 3D Digitization Free downloadable 3D models with curated thematic collections—numismatics, presidential portraits, Jamestown artifacts, women’s history—built on the open-source Voyager viewer.

Decorative Arts & Americana

  • Winterthur Museum — Collection Database 90,000+ American decorative arts objects (1640–1860): ceramics, furniture, glass, metalwork, silver, and textiles from the premier collection of early American material culture.
  • The Henry Ford — Digital Collections Tens of thousands of digitized artifacts documenting American innovation: Edison’s laboratory equipment, automobiles, agricultural tools, and everyday consumer goods, with advanced search by material, maker, and date.
  • Colonial Williamsburg — eMuseum 70,000+ objects of American and British fine, decorative, mechanical, and folk art (seventeenth–nineteenth centuries): ceramics, furniture, textiles, costumes, tools, firearms, and archaeological finds.
  • Metropolitan Museum of Art — Open Access Collection 492,000+ CC0 public-domain images including the American Wing’s galleries of American painting, sculpture, and decorative arts, with high-resolution downloads and an open API.
  • Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum 200,000+ design objects searchable by period, country, color, or size, with open collection data and a public API documenting American design history.
  • Shelburne Museum — Collections 100,000+ items of American folk art and decorative arts across four centuries: quilts, decoys, carousel animals, carriages, toys, and circus memorabilia housed across 39 historic buildings.
  • MESDA — Museum of Early Southern Decorative Arts Object Database Approximately 100,000 photographs documenting nearly 20,000 objects made in the American South before 1861—furniture, ceramics, silver, textiles—compiled through field visits to 11,000+ homes since 1972. (See also Southeast.)

Archaeology as Primary Source

  • DAACS — Digital Archaeological Archive of Comparative Slavery Queryable database of archaeological assemblages from 40+ plantation sites in the Chesapeake, Carolinas, and Caribbean—beads, ceramics, buttons, tools—primary sources for the lives of enslaved people. (See also Slavery & Abolition.)
  • tDAR — the Digital Archaeological Record 411,000+ resources—reports, images, datasets, GIS files—from US and international archaeological investigations, maintained by Digital Antiquity at Arizona State University.
  • Florida Museum — Historical Archaeology Collections 2 million+ excavated specimens from 100+ sites (1492–1900), including the nation’s largest systematic Spanish colonial collection, with a digital ceramic type gallery searchable by paste, glaze, and motif.
  • Jamestown Rediscovery — Artifacts Selected artifacts from the 3 million+ objects recovered since 1994 at America’s first permanent English settlement: exchange goods, household wares, and craft tools browsable by category and excavation context. (See also Colonial America.)

Everyday Things: Textiles, Gravestones & Trade Catalogs

  • The Quilt Index 50,000+ quilts from documentation projects, museums, and private collections (early nineteenth century to present), searchable by pattern, maker, location, and period—run by Michigan State University.
  • Farber Gravestone Collection 13,500+ images of 9,000+ New England gravestones, mostly pre-1800, searchable by carver, iconography, material, and inscription—hosted by the American Antiquarian Society. (See also New England.)
  • Smith College — Historic Clothing Collection 4,000+ pieces of women’s clothing and accessories from all social classes (1800 to present), documenting American dress history through everyday garments as well as high fashion.
  • Smithsonian Libraries — Trade Literature Collection Nearly 500,000 catalogs, brochures, and price lists from almost 40,000 companies—with roughly a million pages digitized (1820–1926)—a key source for the history of consumption, marketing, and industrial design.

Technology & Industry

  • Smithsonian — Patent Models Collection 10,000+ patent models (1836–1910) retained after the US Patent Office model collection was dispersed: carding machines, looms, sewing machines, and inventions of the industrial age, many 3D-scanned and CC0.
  • National Air and Space Museum — Collections 29,000+ searchable objects spanning the history of flight: aircraft, rockets, spacecraft, and instruments, including the 1903 Wright Flyer and the Apollo 11 command module.

Money & Mail

  • Smithsonian — National Numismatic Collection Approximately 1.6 million coins, paper currency, medals, and financial instruments—one of the world’s largest money collections—documenting American economic life from colonial scrip forward. (See also Economic History.)
  • National Postal Museum — Search the Collection 300,000+ objects including nearly every US stamp ever issued, plus mail vehicles, uniforms, and sorting equipment documenting the postal system that knit the country together.

3D Heritage & Historic Sites

  • CyArk Free high-resolution 3D digital twins of at-risk heritage sites created with LiDAR and photogrammetry: US projects include Mount Rushmore, the Lincoln Memorial, Manzanar and Topaz incarceration camps, Taos Pueblo, and Angel Island Immigration Station.

Folk Art & Native Material Culture

  • National Museum of the American Indian — Collections Search 800,000+ objects and photographs documenting Native communities across the Americas, browsable by object type and culture area, with sensitivity protocols honoring tribal restrictions. (See also Native Nations.)
  • American Folk Art Museum — Collection Searchable database of self-taught and folk American art from the eighteenth century to the present: paintings, quilts, samplers, weathervanes, carousel animals, trade signs, and painted furniture.
