Cartographic and landscape records: how Americans measured, divided, claimed, and pictured the land—and how the land itself changed.

Major Map Collections

  • Library of Congress — Geography & Map Division One of the world’s largest map collections: Sanborn fire insurance maps, Civil War maps, panoramic views, and general historical maps from the 1500s forward, freely searchable and downloadable.
  • David Rumsey Map Collection 150,000+ maps, atlases, globes, and views spanning the sixteenth through twenty-first centuries—one of the largest private map collections in the world, freely browsable in high resolution with georeferenced overlays.
  • USGS — topoView 178,000+ historical topographic maps printed 1884–2006, viewable on an interactive index map and downloadable as GeoPDF, GeoTIFF, JPEG, or KMZ—the changing face of the American landscape sheet by sheet.

Surveying the Public Domain

  • Bureau of Land Management — General Land Office Records 5 million+ original land title records, survey plats, and field notes documenting the transfer of public lands into private ownership from the 1780s onward, searchable by name, location, and legal description. (See also Westward Expansion.)
  • Newberry Library — Atlas of Historical County Boundaries Comprehensive digital atlas mapping the creation and boundary changes of every US county from 1629 to 2000, with downloadable GIS shapefiles and chronologies by state.
  • IPUMS NHGIS — National Historical GIS Historical census summary tables and GIS boundary files for population, housing, agriculture, and economic data from 1790 to the present, at state, county, tract, and block levels. (See also Economic History.)
  • Texas General Land Office — Historic Texas Maps Approximately 45,000 digitized historical maps dating to 1721: original land grant surveys, county maps, and township plats searchable by title, date, and feature. (See also Texas.)

Picturing Towns & Cities

  • Library of Congress — Sanborn Fire Insurance Maps Town-by-town maps of approximately 12,000 US communities (1867–1970s) showing building footprints, land use, and infrastructure—the key source for reconstructing historic ground conditions. (See also Built Environment.)
  • Library of Congress — Panoramic Maps Nearly 1,900 bird’s-eye view maps of US and Canadian cities and towns (mostly 1860s–1920s), the promotional cartography that pictured American urban ambition from an imagined elevation.

The View from Above

  • USGS — EarthExplorer Search and download portal for historical aerial photography (1930s onward), satellite imagery, and remote-sensing data covering the entire United States, free to registered users.
  • UC Santa Barbara — Aerial Photography Collection 2.5 million+ aerial images dating from 1924, one of the largest academic collections in the country, concentrated in California but covering most states, searchable via the FrameFinder tool.

Coasts, Rails & Soils

  • NOAA Office of Coast Survey — Historical Charts Thousands of historical nautical charts of US coastlines and harbors, some dating to the early 1800s, produced by the Coast and Geodetic Survey and its predecessors.
  • Library of Congress — Railroad Maps, 1828–1900 Approximately 700 maps documenting the growth of the American railroad network: route maps, land-grant maps, and maps of operating and proposed lines. (See also Gilded Age.)
  • USDA NRCS — Historical Soil Surveys County soil survey manuscripts published 1899–2005, combining detailed soil maps with land-use narratives—an underused record of American agricultural landscapes. (See also Foodways.)

Mapping Native Land

  • Native Land Digital Interactive, community-sourced map of Indigenous territories, languages, and treaties across North America, maintained by an Indigenous-led nonprofit with an open API. (See also Native Nations.)
  • University of Richmond — American Panorama Interactive historical atlas including “Invasion of America,” a time-lapse map of nearly 500 treaty and executive-order land cessions from Native nations (1776–1887) covering roughly 1.5 billion acres, plus maps of redlining, migration, and urban renewal.

War & Cartography

  • Library of Congress — Civil War Maps Approximately 2,240 maps and atlases documenting troop movements, fortifications, and campaign geography, including the Hotchkiss and Sherman map collections. (See also Civil War & Reconstruction.)
  • National Archives — Cartographic Records Federal cartographic holdings including GLO survey plats, USGS records, and government-produced maps and field notes tied to federal land administration, searchable in the National Archives Catalog.
