Home › Civil War & Reconstruction, 1861–1877

Sources for the war, emancipation, and the contested reconstruction of the nation. See also Slavery & Abolition, Military & Wartime, and the Southeast regional page.

Secession & Confederate Records

  • Library of Congress — Confederate States of America Records State, Treasury, War, Navy, Justice, and Post Office department records (bulk 1861–1865), Jefferson Davis’s proclamations, congressional acts, and secession documents, with correspondence of Judah P. Benjamin and other Confederate officials.
  • Digital History — Secession Ordinances Full text of the official ordinances by which each Confederate state severed ties with the Union in 1860–1861.

The Official Records

  • HathiTrust — War of the Rebellion: Official Records The complete 128-volume official compilation of Civil War military records—orders, reports, and correspondence from Union and Confederate commands—fully digitized and full-text searchable.

Soldiers’ Letters & Diaries

  • The Valley of the Shadow Thousands of letters, diaries, maps, and newspaper accounts from two communities—Augusta County, Virginia, and Franklin County, Pennsylvania—tracing secession through Reconstruction on both sides of the line.
  • Duke University — Civil War Soldiers’ Diaries and Letters Digitized diaries and correspondence documenting camp life, combat, and personal reflection, from the Rubenstein Rare Book & Manuscript Library.
  • Auburn University — Civil War Diaries and Letters Digitized Southern soldiers’ and civilians’ diaries and letters (1861–1865) covering military service, home-front hardship, and postwar adjustment.

Civil War Photography

  • Library of Congress — Civil War Glass Negatives 7,000+ original glass plate negatives by Mathew Brady, Alexander Gardner, and Timothy O’Sullivan, freely downloadable in high resolution, including Gardner’s complete Photographic Sketch Book of the War.
  • Library of Congress — Brady-Handy Collection Glass negative portraits from Mathew Brady’s studio of prominent Civil War-era political and military figures.

Lincoln & Emancipation

  • Library of Congress — Abraham Lincoln Papers Approximately 20,000 documents including inaugural address drafts, the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation draft, Gettysburg Address copies, and full presidential correspondence.
  • National Archives — Emancipation Proclamation Digitized images of the preliminary (September 22, 1862) and final (January 1, 1863) Emancipation Proclamations, with transcriptions and historical background.

Black Soldiers & the USCT

  • National Archives — United States Colored Troops Service Records Nearly 3.8 million digitized images of compiled military service records for the roughly 180,000 Black soldiers of the USCT regiments (1863–1866), free to search and view. (See also Slavery & Abolition.)
  • USCT.org — Database and Archive Searchable database compiling USCT soldiers’ service, pension, and regimental records alongside digitized primary documents tracing individual soldiers’ histories.

The Home Front & Women

  • Duke University — Civil War Women Digitized manuscript collections including the Ella Gertrude Clanton Thomas diaries (1848–1889)—a Georgia plantation mistress’s war and Reconstruction account—documenting Southern women’s wartime experiences. (See also Women’s History.)
  • Tennessee Virtual Archive — Women in the Civil War Digitized diaries and letters of Tennessee women, including accounts of the Union occupation of Nashville, offering firsthand female perspectives on the home front.

Maps & Battlefields

  • Library of Congress — Civil War Maps Approximately 2,240 maps and 76 atlases, including the 341-map Hotchkiss Collection (Stonewall Jackson’s topographer) and the Sherman Map Collection, plus 600 maps from Virginia repositories.

Medicine & Hospitals

  • National Museum of Civil War Medicine Resources and digitized records on Civil War medical, surgical, and nursing practices, including the Pry House Field Hospital and Clara Barton’s Missing Soldiers Office.
  • National Library of Medicine — Digital Collections Civil War-era medical texts, surgeons’ reports, and hospital records documenting military medicine, freely searchable and downloadable.

The Freedmen’s Bureau

  • National Archives — Freedmen’s Bureau Records Guide to Record Group 105 (1865–1872): headquarters files, state assistant commissioner records, field office records, and marriage certificates, with free digitized images via FamilySearch.
  • FamilySearch — Freedmen’s Bureau Records Free digitized and indexed records with 1.8 million names, created in partnership with NARA and the Smithsonian: labor contracts, school reports, and family reunification records.
  • DPLA — The Freedmen’s Bureau Curated Reconstruction-era documents and images illustrating the Bureau’s work in education, labor contracts, and legal aid for formerly enslaved people.

Reconstruction Politics & Freedmen’s Schools

  • Tulane — American Missionary Association Archives Records of the AMA, which founded 500+ schools for freedpeople: missionary reports on curricula and conditions in the Reconstruction South, plus materials on the founding of Fisk, Hampton, and Atlanta Universities.

Reconstruction Violence & the Klan Hearings

  • HathiTrust — Ku Klux Klan Hearings (1871) All thirteen parts of the Joint Select Committee report on conditions in the insurrectionary states: fully searchable testimony from 500+ witnesses on Reconstruction-era violence.
  • Bowdoin College — KKK Hearings Guide Access point and volume list for the complete 1871 testimony in the Congressional Serial Set, linked to digitized HathiTrust volumes.

Memory, Veterans & Pensions

  • Library of Congress — Grand Army of the Republic Guide to GAR general orders, encampment proceedings, and post records documenting Union veterans’ pension advocacy, memorial culture, and fraternal life.
  • National Archives — Civil War Pensions & Service Records Guide to Union and Confederate pension files, compiled service records, and veterans’ claims documenting soldiers’ postwar lives and the growth of the pension system.
