Online Sources for United States History
Home › Cold War America, 1945–1991

Sources from the postwar settlement through the fall of the Soviet Union: containment abroad, transformation at home. See also Military & Wartime and US & Latin America.

Truman, Containment & the Marshall Plan

  • Truman Library — The Truman Doctrine Digitized State Department memoranda, diplomatic telegrams from Greece and Turkey, drafts of Truman’s March 1947 speech to Congress, oral histories, and White House photographs (1946–1952).
  • Truman Library — The Marshall Plan 52 documents (441 pages, 1946–1953) documenting the European Recovery Program: State Department memoranda, correspondence, photographs, and oral histories from postwar aid planning.
  • Truman Library — NSC-68 The foundational April 1950 Cold War strategy document within the President’s Secretary’s Files, part of the Ideological Foundations of the Cold War collection.
  • Wilson Center — Digital Archive Tens of thousands of declassified documents translated from Russian, Chinese, and Eastern Bloc archives, plus US materials: containment policy, the Korean War, and Sino-Soviet relations, with visualization tools.

McCarthyism & HUAC

  • National Archives — HUAC Records Guide to 1,245 feet of House Un-American Activities Committee records (1945–1975): correspondence, executive-session transcripts, and investigative files on the Hollywood Blacklist and Alger Hiss, with links to digitized hearing transcripts.

The Korean War

  • National Archives — Korean War Records Casualty lists, searchable MIA/POW databases, and records on African American service members from the 1950–1953 conflict. (See also Military & Wartime.)

The Nuclear Age & Civil Defense

  • DPLA — The Atomic Bomb and the Nuclear Age Curated set (1945–1963): Trinity test photographs, Truman’s authorization, the “Duck and Cover” film, Cuban Missile Crisis transcripts, and the Limited Test Ban Treaty.
  • Cornell — Nuclear Fallout Shelter and Safety Collection Digitized federal and state civil defense pamphlets and newsletters (1959–1970), including “Ten for Survival,” a circular bomb-effects computer, and fallout shelter maps.

Eisenhower & the Interstate Era

  • Eisenhower Library — Interstate Highway System Presidential messages to Congress and staff memoranda surrounding the 1956 Federal Aid Highway Act, with photographs of bill signings. (See also Economic History.)

The Civil Rights Movement

  • Eisenhower Library — Little Rock Integration Crisis Telegrams between Eisenhower and Governor Faubus, notes on deploying the 101st Airborne, Executive Order 10730, correspondence with the Little Rock Nine’s parents, and audio of the national address (1957–1958).
  • Civil Rights Digital Library Portal aggregating civil rights primary sources nationwide, including photograph collections and a digital video archive of historical news film. (See also African American Experience.)
  • SNCC Digital Gateway — Primary Sources Curated directory linking major civil rights digital archives: Duke’s SNCC collections, the 30,000-page Freedom Summer Digital Collection, LOC Civil Rights History Project interviews, and the Take Stock photography collection.

Kennedy, Johnson & the Great Society

  • JFK Library — Digitized Collections Approximately 200,000 scanned pages, 300 audiotapes, 1,500 photographs, and 72 film reels from 400+ collections covering Kennedy’s presidency, foreign policy, and the space program.
  • LBJ Library — DiscoverLBJ Digital library drawing on 45 million+ pages, 650,000 photos, 12,000 hours of audio, and 2,000+ oral histories, including Lady Bird Johnson’s White House diary and Great Society materials.
  • NASA — Apollo Lunar Surface & Flight Journals Document-based accounts of all crewed Apollo flights (1968–1972): mission transcripts, air-to-ground communications, and technical documentation of lunar operations.

The Vietnam War

  • National Archives — The Pentagon Papers The complete, unredacted 2011 release of the Defense Department’s Vietnam Task Force report: approximately 7,000 declassified pages covering 1940–1968.
  • Texas Tech — Virtual Vietnam Archive 1,100+ oral history interviews and approximately 900,000 database records: documents, photographs, maps, moving images, and sound recordings from the Vietnam Center & Sam Johnson Vietnam Archive. (See also Military & Wartime.)

Watergate

  • American Archive of Public Broadcasting — The Watergate Hearings Fifty-one days of PBS gavel-to-gavel Senate Watergate hearing broadcasts (1973) and House impeachment sessions (1974), with searchable video of Dean, Haldeman, Butterfield, and Barbara Jordan.
  • Nixon Library — White House Tapes Digitized secret Nixon White House recordings—part of roughly 3,700 hours of tape—including the Watergate “Smoking Gun” conversation.

The 1970s: Energy, Environment & the Women’s Movement

  • Digital Documerica Nearly 16,000 public-domain EPA DOCUMERICA photographs (1971–1977) documenting pollution and energy-crisis-era America, digitized from the National Archives and searchable by photographer, location, and topic.
  • Duke — Women’s Liberation Movement Print Culture Manifestos, speeches, flyers, and planning documents from 1960s–70s feminist activism, including the Miss America pageant protests, fully text-searchable. (See also Women’s History.)

The Reagan Era & the End of the Cold War

  • Reagan Library — Archives 700+ White House staff and office collections (1967–1989), speeches, WHORM subject files, and an audiovisual archive of 1.6 million photographs.
  • CIA FOIA Reading Room — Reagan Collection 200+ declassified intelligence assessments, National Intelligence Estimates, and DCI talking points on US-Soviet relations in the 1980s.

Declassified Intelligence

  • CIA — CREST 25-Year Program Archive 775,000+ declassified files and 13 million pages of Cold War-era intelligence assessments, cables, and memos, full-text searchable without an account.
  • National Security Archive The world’s largest nongovernmental collection of declassified documents: thousands of Cold War briefing books on Vietnam, nuclear policy, and US-Soviet relations. (See also US & Latin America.)
