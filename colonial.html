Online Sources for United States History
Home › Colonial America, 1492–1763

Sources for the era of European contact, colonization, and the colonial societies of British, Dutch, French, and Spanish North America. See also state pages for New England and the Mid-Atlantic, and thematic pages on Native Nations and Religious History.

European Contact & Exploration

  • Library of Congress — Exploring the Early Americas Exhibition drawing on the 3,000+ item Jay I. Kislak Collection of rare maps, documents, and artifacts (1492–1800s), including the 1507 Waldseemüller world map—the first to name “America”—covering Native American cultures and European contact.
  • American Journeys: Eyewitness Accounts of Early American Exploration Wisconsin Historical Society digital library of 181 firsthand narratives (Viking sagas circa 1000 CE through the early 1800s): 18,000+ pages of searchable rare books, manuscripts, and travel journals organized by region and expedition.
  • Yale Avalon Project — 17th Century Documents Full-text colonial charters, patents, and founding instruments: the Virginia and Massachusetts Bay charters, the Mayflower Compact, the Dutch West India Company charter, and dozens of other foundational legal documents.
  • David Rumsey Map Collection 150,000+ digitized maps and atlases (eighteenth–twentieth centuries) with extensive early American cartography, free to browse and download at high resolution.

Jamestown & the Chesapeake

  • Virtual Jamestown Transcribed Virginia Company records, censuses, land patents, runaway servant advertisements, and letters documenting Jamestown and the “Virginia experiment” (1606–1699), built by the Virginia Center for Digital History.
  • Library of Virginia — Virginia Memory Colonial council and House of Burgesses papers, land patents (1623–1774), and a searchable index of approximately 15,000 documents on colonial Virginia held in British repositories.
  • Colonial Williamsburg — Digital Collections 2,000+ issues of the Virginia Gazette (1736–1780), plus manuscripts, estate inventories, and research reports from the Rockefeller Library, freely browsable and downloadable.

New England: Plymouth & Massachusetts Bay

  • Plymouth Colony Archive Project Searchable court records, colony laws, seventeenth-century journals, probate inventories, wills, and town plans documenting Plymouth Colony (1620–1691), with archaeological and material-culture studies.
  • Massachusetts Historical Society — Winthrop Papers Annotated transcriptions of John Winthrop’s correspondence, journal, and family papers from the founding of Massachusetts Bay, digitized from the documentary edition.
  • Massachusetts Historical Society — Collections Online 100,000+ digitized manuscript images including colonial-era journals, letters, and documentary editions spanning the seventeenth through nineteenth centuries.
  • New England’s Hidden Histories 130,000+ pages of colonial-era church records—relations of faith, disciplinary cases, baptisms, and membership rolls—from Congregational churches across New England, digitized by the Congregational Library & Archives. (See also New England.)

New Netherland & Dutch Records

  • New Netherland Institute English translations and research access to 7,000+ pages of seventeenth-century Dutch colonial administrative records (1624–1664)—the world’s largest online collection of original Dutch West India Company North American documents.
  • New York State Archives — Dutch Colonial Records Digitized images and translations of the Records of New Netherland government series: original seventeenth-century manuscripts browsable and downloadable in high resolution. (See also Mid-Atlantic.)

Colonial Newspapers & Print Culture

  • Library of Congress — Chronicling America Searchable historic newspaper pages from 1756 forward, plus a US Newspaper Directory covering titles published from 1690 (Publick Occurrences) to the present.
  • Library of Virginia — Virginia Chronicle 1.14 million+ digitized Virginia newspaper pages including eighteenth-century colonial titles, full-text searchable and browsable by title and date.
  • Evans Early American Imprint Collection 5,000 fully searchable texts—books, pamphlets, broadsides, and periodicals published in America 1639–1800, based on Charles Evans’s American Bibliography—including sermons, almanacs, and colonial women’s writings.

Salem Witch Trials & Colonial Law

  • University of Virginia — Salem Witch Trials Documentary Archive Transcriptions and images of the primary legal records of the 1692 Salem witchcraft prosecutions: court examinations, indictments, depositions, and petitions.
  • Massachusetts Archives Collection Permanent court and government records dating 1629–1860, including colonial-era criminal and civil case files, probate records, and docket books.

The French & Indian War

  • Fort Ticonderoga — Online Collections Searchable database of 200,000+ artifacts and images documenting military culture of the Atlantic world (circa 1609–1815), with especially deep holdings on the French and Indian War campaigns around Fort Carillon/Ticonderoga.

The Transatlantic Slave Trade

  • SlaveVoyages Records of 36,000+ transatlantic and 11,000 intra-American slave-trading voyages (1514–1866): searchable ship manifests, personal-name records, and interactive maps documenting the forced migration of enslaved Africans. (See also Slavery & Abolition.)
  • Slavery Images: A Visual Record Approximately 1,280 prints, paintings, and photographs documenting the Atlantic slave trade and slavery in the Americas, organized by Jerome Handler and Michael Tuite.

Indigenous–Colonial Relations

  • National Archives — American Indian Treaties Digitized original treaties between colonial governments, the United States, and Native nations, plus treaty-council minutes and commissioner instructions from Record Group 75. (See also Native Nations.)
  • Yale Avalon Project — Treaties with Native Nations Free full-text collection of Indigenous-colonial treaties and related statutes from the colonial and early federal periods.

The Great Awakening

  • Yale — Works of Jonathan Edwards Online 73 digital volumes of Edwards’s sermons, notebooks, essays, letters, and personal writings from the First Great Awakening (1730s–1750s), fully searchable by chronology, theme, and scripture reference. (See also Religious History.)

Major Cross-Era Collections

  • Digital Public Library of America 53 million+ free images, texts, and records aggregated from US libraries, archives, and museums, with curated colonial-era primary source sets and browsable holdings from state hubs.
  • Internet Archive — Colonial Records of Virginia Full-text scan of the Virginia General Assembly’s published compilation of official correspondence and legislative records of the Virginia colony.
