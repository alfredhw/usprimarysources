Sources from the imperial crisis through the War of 1812: independence, constitution-making, and the first decades of the republic. See also the Military & Wartime theme and Mid-Atlantic and New England regional pages.

The Imperial Crisis

  • Yale Avalon Project — 18th Century Documents Full-text collection of the Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, Boston Port Act, Quebec Act, non-importation agreements, and other imperial-crisis documents (1764 onward), alongside state constitutions and ratification records.
  • Massachusetts Historical Society — Boston Massacre Trials John Adams’s original trial notes from Rex v. Preston and Rex v. Wemms, witness testimony, and the printed 1770 trial account, cross-linked to the Adams Papers Digital Edition.

Continental Congress & the Declaration

  • Library of Congress — Journals of the Continental Congress The 34-volume Journals of the Continental Congress (1774–1789), Letters of Delegates to Congress, Elliot’s Debates, and Farrand’s Records, from the Century of Lawmaking collection.
  • National Archives — America’s Founding Documents High-resolution images and transcripts of the Declaration of Independence, Constitution, and Bill of Rights, with drafting history and signer biographies.

Revolutionary War Military Records

  • National Archives — American Revolution Research Guide Military service and pension records, muster rolls, and bounty-land warrant files for Revolutionary War soldiers (1775–1783), with citizen-transcription projects for pension files.
  • Southern Campaigns Revolutionary War Pension Statements Thousands of transcribed veteran pension applications and rosters from Georgia, the Carolinas, and Virginia, sourced from NARA microfilm and state archives, fully searchable and citation-ready.
  • Papers of the War Department, 1784–1800 Reconstruction of the War Department’s files (destroyed by fire in 1800) from copies in other archives: military affairs, pensions, and Indigenous diplomacy, with crowdsourced transcriptions. (See also Military & Wartime.)

The Founders’ Papers

  • Founders Online 184,000+ fully searchable, annotated documents by Washington, Franklin, Adams, Jefferson, Hamilton, Jay, and Madison, spanning the colonial period through 1836—the National Archives’ flagship documentary database.
  • Library of Congress — George Washington Papers Approximately 77,000 items (1745–1799), the world’s largest Washington archive: French and Indian War service, Continental Army command, and the presidency, with crowdsourced transcriptions.
  • Library of Congress — Thomas Jefferson Papers Roughly 27,000 documents including drafts of the Declaration of Independence and correspondence spanning Jefferson’s presidency and the Louisiana Purchase negotiations.
  • Library of Congress — Alexander Hamilton Papers Approximately 12,000 items (1777–1804): Constitutional Convention notes, Federalist drafts, and Treasury Department records, fully digitized and public domain.
  • The Papers of Benjamin Franklin Free searchable digital edition of Franklin’s correspondence and writings (1706–1790), sponsored by the American Philosophical Society and Yale, covering diplomacy, science, and the Convention.

The Constitution & Ratification

  • Yale Avalon Project — The American Constitution: A Documentary Record Madison’s Notes on the Federal Convention, Farrand’s Records, state ratification proceedings, and convention delegate papers (1787–1791), all free full text.
  • Yale Avalon Project — The Federalist Papers Complete text of all 85 Federalist essays (1787–1788) by Hamilton, Madison, and Jay, alongside contemporaneous state-by-state ratification debate transcripts.
  • Documentary History of the Ratification of the Constitution University of Wisconsin–Madison’s digitized edition of the 41-volume series compiling state ratification debates, correspondence, and Federalist/Antifederalist newspaper essays (1776–1791).
  • Teaching American History — Document Library Assembles the scattered body of Antifederalist essays, speeches, and pamphlets opposing ratification (1787–1788) alongside Federalist counterparts and founding-era core documents.

The Early Republic

  • Library of Congress — American State Papers The 38-volume, 6,278-document compilation of congressional legislative and executive papers (1789–1838), organized into ten classes including Foreign Relations, Indian Affairs, and Military Affairs.
  • A New Nation Votes: American Election Returns, 1787–1825 Searchable database of 18,000+ local, state, and federal election returns compiled over forty years by Philip Lampi, covering presidential, congressional, and gubernatorial races with party labels and source citations.
  • National Archives — Louisiana Purchase Treaty Transcript and background on the 1803 Treaty of Cession and its financial conventions, documenting the $15 million purchase that doubled the nation’s territory. (See also Westward Expansion.)
  • Library of Congress — Chronicling America Searchable early republic newspapers (1789–1815) covering partisan politics, the Whiskey Rebellion, and the War of 1812.

The War of 1812

  • Library of Congress — War of 1812 Resource Guide Curated guide aggregating manuscripts, broadsides, congressional debates, prints, and newspaper coverage of the War of 1812 (1812–1815) across the Library’s digital collections.

The Early Supreme Court

  • National Archives — Records of the Supreme Court Finding aids and digitized case files for the earliest Supreme Court records (1790s onward): minutes, dockets, and attorney rolls from the Court’s founding years.
  • Internet Archive — Supreme Court Appellate Case File Index, 1792–1909 Digitized card index enabling researchers to locate early Supreme Court appellate case files by party name across the Court’s first century.
